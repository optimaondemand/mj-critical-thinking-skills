Reading the Lines — Resolving Ambiguities
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
When something can mean more than one thing, how do you figure out what it actually means?
Have you ever heard a sentence and thought, "Wait, what does that mean exactly?" That's what we call ambiguity — when something can have more than one meaning. "The star is shining brightly." Are they talking about a celebrity or the sun? The process of figuring out the right meaning is called disambiguation. Authors, speakers, and advertisers use ambiguity all the time — sometimes by accident, sometimes on purpose. Today you'll learn how to spot it and resolve it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to spot ambiguous statements and explain why they're ambiguous.
2
I will be able to disambiguate using context, questions, and the author's purpose.
3
I will be able to identify "deepities" — statements that sound profound but turn out to be either trivial or false.
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
"I saw her duck." — what's the first interpretation that hits you?
📚 PART 1 · FOUR REASONS AMBIGUITY EXISTS
  1. Accident. The speaker didn't realize the wording could be read two ways. "I watched her duck."
  2. Ignorance. The person doesn't fully understand what they're talking about. "The brain is a computer." — how is it like a computer, exactly?
  3. Poetic or comedic effect. The ambiguity is the point. "The peasants are revolting" — disgusting, or in rebellion? Maybe both.
  4. Malicious intent. Someone wants to manipulate. "This product is guaranteed to make you happier." Happier than what?
📚 PART 2 · HOW TO DISAMBIGUATE
  1. Look for context clues. "The star is shining brightly at the concert." Now we know it's a celebrity.
  2. Ask questions. "Are you talking about an animal, or did she crouch down?"
  3. Consider the author's purpose. Are they being poetic? Simplifying? Trying to manipulate you?
📚 PART 3 · DEEPITIES — PROFUNDITIES SPEWED

A deepity (a term coined by philosopher Daniel Dennett) is a statement that sounds profound but, when you look closely, breaks into two layers:

  1. One interpretation that is profound… but false.
  2. Another interpretation that is true… but trivial.
"Love is just a word."
— Trivial: "love" is literally a word in the dictionary. True but obvious.
— Profound but false: love is nothing more than a word — ignoring all the emotions, experiences, and relationships tied to it.
"Beauty is only skin deep."
— Trivially true: beauty is often judged on appearance.
— Profoundly false: it suggests beauty has no deeper significance — ignoring emotional, cultural, spiritual, moral aspects of beauty.
"Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic."
— Trivially true: people CAN be fooled into thinking advanced tech is magic.
— Profoundly false: a sufficiently advanced technology would BE magic. We know it's not magic precisely because we know it's tech.

Challenge: See if you can show why "A machine running a sufficiently advanced computer program really is intelligent" is just as much a deepity as the magic one.

⚡ TRY IT · DEEPITY OR NOT?
For each statement, decide: is it a deepity (sounds profound but breaks down into trivial-OR-false), or a genuine claim?
"Everything happens for a reason."
"Regular exercise lowers blood pressure on average."
"Time is an illusion."
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND DEEPITIES
AI assistants often produce deepities by accident — they're trained on a LOT of writing that sounds wise, and they can stitch together a sentence that sounds profound without actually meaning anything specific. When you read an AI's answer, run the deepity check: does it break into a trivial reading and a false-but-impressive reading? If yes, it's a deepity. Ask for a specific, falsifiable claim instead.

💪 THE TAKEAWAY

Disambiguation and understanding deepities are crucial skills for clear thinking. When you can see through ambiguity and break down seemingly profound statements, you become a more effective communicator and thinker. Next time you come across something that sounds confusing or too good to be true, don't just accept it. Break it down. Ask: what does this really mean?
