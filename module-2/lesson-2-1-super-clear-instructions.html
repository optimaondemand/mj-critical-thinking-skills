📚 PART 1 · WHAT IS COMMUNICATION, REALLY?

Communication is everything we do that sends a message to someone else — whether we realize it or not. It includes:

  • Verbal communication — The actual words you say (or type).
  • Nonverbal communication — Body language, facial expressions, tone of voice, and even the way you dress.
  • Intentional communication — Messages you purposely send (like waving to say hello).
  • Unintentional communication — Messages you send without meaning to (like yawning while someone is talking and making them think you're bored).

For example, imagine I never tell you that I'm married, but you notice a wedding ring on my finger. You've learned something about me — not because I said it, but because of what I showed you. That's nonverbal communication at work.

VERBAL (words)

The trickiest box is bottom-right — nonverbal AND unintentional. That's where most miscommunication hides.
📚 PART 2 · COMMUNICATION GONE WRONG

Because communication includes so much — words, body language, tone of voice, assumptions — it's very easy to miscommunicate. Have you ever:

  • Sent a text that someone misinterpreted?
  • Thought you explained something well, but the person did something totally different?
  • Felt like someone ignored what you said, but they actually just didn't hear you?

These are all examples of unclear communication. Even when we think we're being clear, there are many reasons our message might not come across the way we expect.

📚 PART 3 · WHY ARE WE SOMETIMES UNCLEAR?

1. We assume people already know what we mean.

  • If you say, "Go to that place over there," but your friend doesn't know where "there" is, they won't get it right.
  • Fix it: Be specific. Instead of "that place," say "the bench next to the library entrance."

2. We leave out important steps.

  • Imagine giving someone directions to your house but forgetting to mention a turn they need to take. That missing step will throw everything off.
  • Fix it: Walk through your instructions in your mind as if you were the person following them for the first time.

3. We use vague or confusing words.

  • Saying, "Move that thing over here" doesn't help if someone doesn't know what "thing" or "here" means.
  • Fix it: Use clear, direct words. Instead of "that thing," say "the blue notebook."

4. We think people understand things the same way we do.

  • If you and a friend play the same video game but have different strategies, saying "Go left" might mean one thing to you and something totally different to them.
  • Fix it: Check for understanding. Ask, "Do you know what I mean?" or have them repeat back the steps.

5. Nonverbal signals can change the message.

  • If you say "I'm fine" but roll your eyes and sigh, people will believe your body language more than your words.
  • Fix it: Make sure your tone and expressions match what you mean to say.
📚 PART 4 · HOW TO MAKE YOUR COMMUNICATION CLEARER

Now that we know where communication goes wrong, let's look at how to get it right:

  • Be specific. Instead of "Put this somewhere," say "Put this book on the shelf."
  • Break it down step by step. Imagine explaining it to someone who knows nothing about the topic.
  • Ask for feedback. Say, "Can you repeat that back to me?" to check if they understood.
  • Watch for confusion. If someone looks puzzled, stop and ask if they have questions.
  • Match your words to your actions. If you say "I'm happy to help" but look annoyed, people might believe your tone more than your words.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND CLEAR INSTRUCTIONS
When you give instructions to a person, they fill in gaps with what they already know about you. When you give instructions to an AI, it has only your words — no shared history, no body language, no glance for "did that make sense?" Every skill in this lesson — being specific, breaking it down, watching for confusion — is exactly what makes a strong prompt to an AI tool too. If you can give crystal-clear instructions to a person, you're already most of the way to giving crystal-clear prompts to a machine.

💪 THE TAKEAWAY

Communication isn't just about talking — it's about making sure your message is understood the way you intended. Whether you're explaining how to play a game, giving directions, or just trying to tell a story, using clear, intentional communication will make everything easier.

So next time you explain something, slow down, be specific, and check if the other person really understands — because great communication isn't just about what you say, but how well it's received!
