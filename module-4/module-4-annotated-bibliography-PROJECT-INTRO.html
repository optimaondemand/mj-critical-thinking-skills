Module 4 Project
VR Research Library & Annotated Bibliography
🔊 READ ALOUD — LISTEN TO THIS PAGE
Across eight lessons, you'll build a VR Research Library — your personal annotated workspace for four articles you find yourself on a topic of your own choosing. By the end of the module, your library will be a portfolio piece you can walk a viewer through, and your work inside it becomes an annotated bibliography — the kind of thing real researchers produce before they write a serious paper.
📚 YOUR TOPIC AND YOUR SOURCES

You choose the topic. Pick something you're genuinely curious about — a question you actually want answered, not just one that sounds "school-appropriate." Then you find the sources: four written articles on your topic, each from a different kind of publication. Real researchers don't get handed their sources — finding them IS the research. Your set of four should be intentionally mixed, not four versions of the same viewpoint:

Shelf 1 · Article 1 — a straight news report

A reported news article whose main job is to tell you what happened. You find and choose this article yourself.

Shelf 2 · Article 2 — an opinion or editorial piece

A column, op-ed, or editorial that argues a position on your topic. Someone is trying to convince you of something.

Shelf 3 · Article 3 — an official, expert, or organizational source

Something published by a government agency, research organization, university, or recognized expert — a report, fact sheet, or expert-written article.

Shelf 4 · Article 4 — your choice, but a genuinely different perspective

Any written article you like — as long as it brings a viewpoint, angle, or voice the other three don't already cover.

All four shelves go inside your VR Research Library. You'll annotate every article.

🔍 THE FOUR VARIABLES YOU'LL EVALUATE, EVERY TIME

For each of your 4 articles, you'll evaluate the same four things:

  • Audience — who was this written for?
  • Author — who wrote it, and what's their expertise or likely bias?
  • Purpose — why was this written (to inform, persuade, entertain, sell)?
  • Context — when and where was it published, and what larger conversation or moment is it part of?
🏠 THE 8 STAGES OF YOUR VR RESEARCH LIBRARY

Each lesson adds one station to your library. By Lesson 8 the whole library is built and ready for your capstone tour.

Stage 1 · Library Foyer (Lesson 1: Previewing & Predicting)

Build the shell of your library. Choose a VR space, place a Welcome sticky with your topic, and post your starting predictions for each of your 4 articles.

Stage 2 · Question Wall (Lesson 2: Asking Questions of a Text)

A wall of sticky notes with the questions YOU want your 4 articles to answer. This drives the rest of your reading.

Stage 3 · Source Shelves (Lesson 3: Big Picture and the Details)

Place your 4 articles on labeled shelves. For each article: one sticky with a one-line "big picture" summary, plus your evaluation of the four variables — Audience, Author, Purpose, and Context.

Stage 4 · Direct Claims Board (Lesson 4: Reading the Lines)

For each of your 4 articles, post 2–3 sticky notes listing the DIRECT claims the text makes (what the article straight-out says).

Stage 5 · Inference Board (Lesson 5: Reading between the Lines)

For each of your 4 articles, post 1–2 sticky notes listing the INFERRED claims — what's implied but not stated outright.

Stage 6 · Structure Map (Lesson 6: Text Structures & Visuals)

Use the drawing tool to draw each article's structural skeleton (timeline? cause-effect? compare-contrast?). Place each article's key visual in the library as an image.

Stage 7 · Connection Web (Lesson 7: Summarizing and Synthesizing)

Use the drawing tool to draw connections between your 4 articles. Where do they agree? Disagree? What patterns appear?

Stage 8 · Citation Station (Lesson 8: Academic Integrity)

For each of your 4 articles, post a properly formatted citation sticky. Walk the whole library — this is your dress rehearsal for the capstone tour.
🏆 CAPSTONE DELIVERABLE

At the end of the module, you'll submit two things:

  1. The Annotated Bibliography — a Word document with one annotated entry per article (4 total): citation + summary + your evaluation of Audience, Author, Purpose, and Context.
  2. A recorded Library Tour video — a guided walk through your VR library showing all 8 stations and explaining what each shelf contains and how your 4 articles connect.

Both pieces follow research-paper conventions. You're not writing the paper — but you're producing the preparation a real researcher would do BEFORE writing.

⚠️ WHY THIS PROJECT MATTERS FOR AI LITERACY
When you use an AI tool to "find information," the model will hand you sources that look plausible but might be biased, wrong, or even invented. The skills you build in this project — previewing, questioning, inferring, structure-mapping, synthesizing, citing — are exactly what separates someone who can verify what AI tells them from someone who just trusts it. The library you build here is muscle memory for the rest of your life as a reader.
