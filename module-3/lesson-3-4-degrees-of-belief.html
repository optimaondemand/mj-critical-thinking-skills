📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Belief isn't a light switch — it's a dimmer.
Today, we're diving into a topic that will help you understand not just what you believe, but how strongly you believe it. It's about degrees of belief — a fancy way of saying how sure you are that something is true. Ever noticed that sometimes you're absolutely certain about something, while other times you're just leaning towards a conclusion? Let's explore this idea in more detail and see how it connects to the way we think, argue, and make decisions.

📚 PART 1 · TWO WAYS TO THINK ABOUT BELIEF

When you think about your belief in a statement like, "The sun will rise tomorrow," you can have two different types of attitudes:

  1. Binary Belief: This is all or nothing. You either believe that the sun will rise tomorrow, or you don't. It's like a switch that's either on or off.
  2. Graded Belief (aka Credence, aka Confidence): This is more like a dimmer switch. It's not just on or off; it has levels. Maybe you're 99% sure the sun will rise tomorrow, but not 100% (after all, who knows, right?). We call this level of certainty your credence (or confidence level).
📊 THE CREDENCE DIMMER
0% — definitely false
50% — no idea
100% — definitely true

Every belief you hold lives somewhere on this scale. The job of a careful thinker is to place each one in the right spot.
📚 PART 2 · INTERPRETING CREDENCES

So, what does it mean when someone says, "My credence that the sun will rise tomorrow is 0.99"? Roughly, it's their way of saying, "I'm 99% sure this will happen." In a little more detail, there are two main ways people think about what it means to have a credence in a statement:

  1. "I think there's a 99% chance that the sun will rise tomorrow."
  2. "Out of 100 similar situations where I thought like this, I'd expect to be right about 99 times."

Both interpretations help you understand how strongly you believe something. And being clear about this helps us communicate and make decisions better.

📚 PART 3 · WHY GRADED BELIEF IS BETTER

It matches everyday speech

Think about how people talk:

  • "I knew it was going to rain!"
  • "I think she might show up."
  • "That'll happen when pigs fly!"

We rarely speak in binary terms — like saying we absolutely know something or we absolutely don't. Using graded beliefs makes more sense and aligns better with our everyday experience.

It matches our actions

You might believe both that your brakes will work and that a complex scientific theory is true. But here's the thing: you'll bet your life on the brakes (by driving to the gym every day, say), while you probably wouldn't do the same for that scientific theory. This shows that you have a much higher credence in your brakes than in the scientific theory.

If beliefs were only binary, it wouldn't make sense that you're willing to act on one belief and not the other. But with graded beliefs, you can understand the difference: you're more confident in one than the other.

It helps us adjust to new evidence

Think about how people react to new information:

  • If you just believe something, more evidence can't really change your mind.
  • But if you have a credence — a level or degree of confidence — new evidence can shift that confidence up or down.

For example, if you hear a convincing argument from someone on the other side of a political debate, it might make you a little less certain of your own position, even if you don't completely change your mind. This kind of adjustment is impossible with binary beliefs.

📚 PART 4 · WHY USE PROBABILITIES?

This is where it gets a little technical, but let's keep it simple. For now, let's agree that credences work like probabilities. Just like you might say there's a 50% chance a coin flip lands heads, you can say your credence in something happening is 50% (or 0.5).

Why do we use probabilities? Because it gives us a systematic way to handle uncertainty and make decisions based on our beliefs. If you're curious and want to dig deeper, classes in philosophy or epistemology (the study of knowledge) might be up your alley.

📚 PART 5 · HOW TO DETERMINE CREDENCES

So, where do you get the numbers for your credences / confidence levels? How do you decide that you're 70% sure about something? Well, there's a pretty sophisticated way to track your credences that we'll look at in the next few lessons. But for now, here's a very general guide:

  1. Start with Your Total Evidence and Background Knowledge: You use what you know and have experienced. If you've seen cloudy skies all morning, you might have a high credence that it will rain later since, in your experience, rain usually follows cloudy skies.
  2. Update as You Get New Evidence: Let's say you check the weather app and see a 0% chance of rain. This new evidence might lower your credence in rain happening today. The more evidence you collect, the better your credence will reflect reality.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI'S "FALSE CONFIDENCE" PROBLEM
AI assistants speak with confidence even when they're guessing. They almost never say "I'm only 40% sure of this." Always ask the AI: "On a scale of 0 to 100, how confident are you in that answer, and what would lower your confidence?" If it can't give you a credence with a reason, the answer is just a guess in a confident voice.

💪 THE TAKEAWAY

Understanding credence and degrees of belief helps us think more clearly about what we believe and how strongly we believe it. Instead of just saying "I believe this" or "I don't believe that," you can now express how confident you are. This will help you make better decisions, understand different perspectives, and keep your mind open when new evidence comes along.
