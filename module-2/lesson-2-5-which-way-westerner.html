📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Smart Choices — A Simple Way to Pick Wisely
Have you ever had to choose between two good options — and felt stuck? Like whether to join the soccer team or audition for the school play? You can imagine some things going well… and others not so much.

Making a smart decision doesn't mean predicting the future. It means thinking carefully about what could happen, how good or bad those things would be, and how likely they are.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use a five-step decision tool: options → outcomes → value → likelihood → pick.
2
I will be able to apply this tool to a real decision I'm currently facing.

⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Is there a real decision you're currently stuck on?
📚 PART 1 · THE DECISION TOOL (STEP-BY-STEP)
  1. List Your Options. Write down each choice you're considering.
  2. List Possible Outcomes for Each. For each option, write down a few things that could happen — good or bad.
  3. Rate How Good or Bad Each Outcome Is. You can label them simply (you can use these ratings on multiple outcomes):
    • 😊 Great outcome
    • 😐 Okay outcome
    • ☹️ Bad outcome
  4. Estimate How Likely Each Outcome Is. Next to each one, mark (the strength of blue indicates the strength of likeliness; also, you can apply these likelihoods to more than one outcome):
    • Very likely
    • Somewhat likely
    • Unlikely
  5. Pick the Option with the Best Combo. Look for the option that has the best mix: outcomes that are good and more likely to happen. Avoid options with big downsides that are also likely.
⚽ STORY TIME · SOCCER TEAM OR SCHOOL PLAY?

Let's try the system with this common choice.

Option 1: Try out for the Soccer Team
  • 😊 Great: Become a strong player and earn respect from teammates (Somewhat likely)
  • 😐 Okay: Make the team and get to play in most games (Very likely)
  • ☹️ Bad: Get a sprained ankle and miss the season (Unlikely)
Option 2: Audition for the School Play
  • 😊 Great: Get the lead role and impress your classmates (Unlikely)
  • 😐 Okay: Get a small part and enjoy performing (Somewhat likely)
  • ☹️ Bad: Don't get a part and miss the chance to participate (Somewhat likely)

Analysis: The soccer team has a good chance of steady success (making the team), and the best outcome (becoming a strong player) is more likely than the best play outcome. The bad outcome — injury — is possible but not very likely. With the play, the biggest success is harder to get, and the worst case (not being cast) could easily happen.

Conclusion: In this scenario, soccer offers a better mix of good chances and smaller risks. That makes it the smarter bet — not because it's "safe," but because it offers more likely gains with fewer likely losses.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AS A DECISION SOUNDING BOARD
If you ever ask an AI to help you decide between two options, notice what it's actually doing: it's listing possible outcomes and giving them rough probabilities. It's running this exact tool, just faster. The catch: the AI doesn't know your values, what you actually enjoy, or what your specific situation is. Use it to brainstorm outcomes you might not have thought of. Decide which ones matter to you yourself.
⚡ TRY IT · PICK THE BEST COMBO
Given the outcomes and likelihoods, which option is the smarter pick?
1. Two restaurants for a celebration dinner. (A) Trusted favorite — almost certainly a good night, but never amazing. (B) New place — might be incredible, might be a disaster, no way to tell.
2. Friday night plans. (A) Stay home — comfortable, nothing memorable. (B) Try a new local meetup for something you're into — mostly likely you have an OK time, decent chance you meet someone interesting, small chance it's awkward.
3. Shortcut vs. known route to school. (A) Known route — gets you there on time every day. (B) "Faster" shortcut — saves 5 min if it works, but a single locked gate makes you 20 min late.

💪 THE TAKEAWAY

Smart decisions aren't just lucky guesses — they're the result of clear thinking. When you weigh what might happen, how good or bad it is, and how likely it is, you're not just hoping for the best… you're thinking like a strategist. And the more you practice, the better your decisions will get.
