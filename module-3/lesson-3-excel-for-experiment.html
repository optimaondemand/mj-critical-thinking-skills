Tracking Your Experiment in Excel 📊
Setting up your digital data log for the Good Life Experiment, from zero.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
From "what is Excel?" to "I have a working data log."
In Lesson 5, you'll begin collecting daily data on your Good Life Experiment. Before that happens, you need a place to put that data. This lesson assumes you've never used Excel before. We'll start at the beginning — signing in to your school account — and end with a working data log in OneDrive, ready to fill in each day.

Excel is the spreadsheet tool in Microsoft 365 — the same family as Word and PowerPoint. Your school gives you free access to all of it. Scientists, researchers, and anyone running an experiment use spreadsheets to log data because they're easier to analyze and graph than a paper notebook. By the end of today, you'll know exactly how to set yours up.

I will be able to sign in to my school Microsoft 365 account and find OneDrive.
2
I will be able to create, name, and save a new Excel spreadsheet using the Optima file naming rule.

I will be able to set up a data log with column headers, enter daily data, and use basic formulas (COUNT, AVERAGE) and a simple chart.

📚 PART 1 · WHY EXCEL FOR AN EXPERIMENT?

You could log your daily experiment data in a notebook, and that's a perfectly fine companion (the project description even suggests it). But Excel adds two things a notebook can't:

  • Quick math. Once your data is in a spreadsheet, you can ask Excel "What's the average?" or "How many days did I actually do the habit?" and it answers in seconds. You'll need this in Lessons 7 and 8.
  • Charts. A line or bar chart of your data makes patterns visible at a glance. Your final video presentation in this module will look much sharper with a real chart in it.

Think of Excel as the upgrade to your composition notebook for the parts where math and pictures help.

🔒 PART 2 · SIGN IN TO MICROSOFT 365 (FIRST TIME)

Optima gives every student a school Microsoft 365 account. This is different from any personal Microsoft account you (or your family) might have. You must use the school account — otherwise the file naming rules and OneDrive folders won't match up, and your teacher won't be able to see your work.

  1. Open a web browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari — any of them).
  2. Go to office.com (or portal.office.com).
  3. Click Sign in.
  4. Enter your school email address. It'll look something like firstname.lastname@optimaedu.org (your teacher will give you the exact format).
  5. Enter your school password.
  6. You should see a landing page with icons for Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneDrive, and more.
⚠️ WATCH OUT
If you've used Microsoft tools on this computer before with a different account, the browser may try to sign you in with that one automatically. Look at the top-right of the page after signing in — click the circle with your initials and make sure it's your school account. If it isn't, sign out and start over.
☁️ PART 3 · ONEDRIVE — YOUR DIGITAL BINDER

OneDrive is Microsoft's cloud storage. Think of it like a digital binder: every file you create or save lives in OneDrive, and you can get to it from any computer by signing in. Optima expects every student to organize their OneDrive the same way — like keeping the same tabbed binder for every class.

The folder structure you should have:

OneDrive (your school account)
└── Optima/
    ├── Critical Thinking/
    │   └── Critical_Thinking_2025-26/    ← YOU'LL CREATE THIS
    ├── ELA/
    ├── Math/
    ├── Science/
    └── [other subjects]/

To create the Critical Thinking folder:

  1. From the office.com landing page, click the OneDrive icon (it looks like a blue cloud).
  2. Look for an existing folder called Optima. If there isn't one, click + New at the top, then Folder, and name it Optima.
  3. Open the Optima folder.
  4. Click + New → Folder and create one called Critical Thinking.
  5. Open it.
  6. Click + New → Folder again and create one called Critical_Thinking_2025-26 (or whatever school year you're in).

That last folder is where your spreadsheet will live.

📁 PART 4 · CREATE YOUR EXCEL FILE

From inside your Critical_Thinking_2025-26 folder in OneDrive:

  1. Click + New at the top.
  2. Choose Excel workbook. (A "workbook" is just what Excel calls a spreadsheet file.)
  3. Excel opens with a blank sheet. You'll see a grid of cells — rows numbered 1, 2, 3… and columns labeled A, B, C…
  4. At the top, it'll say something like "Book1" or "Document". Click that name to rename the file.
📝 PART 5 · NAME YOUR FILE (THE OPTIMA WAY)

Every file you save in Optima follows the same naming rule. This is so your teacher (who has 500 students) can find your work fast, and so you can find your own work later.

LastName_FirstName_AssignmentName_CourseCode

For this experiment data log, type this (replacing the bold parts with your own info):

LastName_FirstName_GoodLifeDataLog_MJCRT

For example, if Jane Smith is doing this experiment, her file would be named: Smith_Jane_GoodLifeDataLog_MJCRT

Use underscores ( _ ), no spaces, no special characters. Capital first letters keep the parts easy to read.

Excel saves your file to OneDrive automatically as you work — no need to remember to "save." This is one of the best things about working in M365: you can't lose your work.

📝 PART 6 · SET UP YOUR DATA LOG

A spreadsheet is just a grid. Rows go across (numbered 1, 2, 3…) and columns go down (lettered A, B, C…). The little boxes are cells. You click a cell and type into it.

For your data log, you'll use the first row as the column headers (what each column means) and one row per day for your data.

Click cell A1 (top-left). Type Date and press Tab. Now you're in cell B1. Continue across the row, typing one header per cell:

📝 SAMPLE DATA LOG — SLEEP & FOCUS EXPERIMENT
  A B C D E F
1 Date Did the habit? Hours of sleep Focus rating (1-10) Mood rating (1-10) Notes
2 10/14 Yes 8.5 8 7 Felt sharp in math
3 10/15 Yes 9 9 8 Best day so far
4 10/16 No 6 5 5 Stayed up late
5 10/17 Yes 8 7 7 Felt better than 10/16
Five columns is plenty. Pick measurements that fit your specific experiment — you decided what to measure back in Lesson 3 (Methodology Wall).

Pick columns that match your own experiment. Examples by experiment type:

  • Gratitude: Date / Did 3 gratitudes? / Mood (1-10) / Stress (1-10) / Notes
  • Phone-Free Hour: Date / Did the phone-free hour? / Focus tonight (1-10) / Sleep quality (1-10) / Notes
  • Outdoor Time: Date / Minutes outside / Mood (1-10) / Energy (1-10) / Notes
  • Listening: Date / Practiced active listening? / How conversations went (1-10) / Notes

If your experiment is different, adapt the columns. The first column should always be Date. The last column should always be Notes.

📅 PART 7 · DAILY ENTRY (3 MINUTES A DAY)

Each day of your experiment, open your data log and add one row. Three minutes, tops. Best done at the same time daily — right before bed works for most people because you can rate the whole day.

  1. Open OneDrive (or office.com → Excel → recent files).
  2. Open your data log file.
  3. Click the next empty cell in column A (under your last date).
  4. Type today's date in the format 10/14 or 10/14/25 (be consistent every day).
  5. Press Tab to move to the next column. Fill in each cell.
  6. Done. The file auto-saves.
🧮 PART 8 · SIMPLE FORMULAS (USE THESE IN LESSON 7)

Once you have a week or two of data, you can ask Excel some questions. A formula is a special instruction in a cell that starts with the equals sign =. You type the formula in an empty cell and Excel calculates the answer.

=AVERAGE() — What's my typical day?

If your sleep hours are in column C, rows 2 through 15, click an empty cell and type:

=AVERAGE(C2:C15)

Press Enter. Excel gives you the average sleep across those 14 days.

=COUNTIF() — How many days did I actually do it?

If "Did the habit?" is in column B with "Yes" or "No" entries:

=COUNTIF(B2:B15,"Yes")

Excel counts how many cells in that range say "Yes."

=MAX() and =MIN() — Best and worst days

For your mood rating in column E:

=MAX(E2:E15)
=MIN(E2:E15)

Highest and lowest mood scores across the experiment.

📈 PART 9 · MAKE A CHART (USE THIS IN YOUR FINAL VIDEO)

A chart lets anyone watching your final video see your results at a glance. Excel will build one in three clicks.

  1. Click the top-left cell of your data (probably A1, the "Date" header).
  2. Hold Shift and click the bottom-right cell of your data (the last cell with anything in it).
  3. In the toolbar at the top, click Insert → Chart (or just Chart on the web version).
  4. Pick a Line chart for trends over time, or a Bar chart for comparisons.
  5. The chart appears. Drag it where you want it. Excel saves automatically.
