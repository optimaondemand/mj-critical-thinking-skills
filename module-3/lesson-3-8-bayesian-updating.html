Lesson 8
Bayesian Updating — Changing Your Mind, Carefully
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Update what you believe by combining what you knew before with what the evidence tells you now.
Welcome, critical thinkers! Today we're combining everything we've learned into one powerful tool for refining your beliefs when new evidence arrives. The tool is called Bayesian Updating, named after the 18th-century mathematician Reverend Thomas Bayes. It's the system science, spam filters, and AI use to "learn" from new information — consistently, logically, and at scale.

2
I will be able to walk through one Bayesian update by hand, multiplying priors by a likelihood ratio.
3
I will be able to explain why strong evidence alone isn't enough — priors matter too.

📚 PART 1 · A LITTLE HISTORY

Thomas Bayes came up with this rule in the 1700s, but it wasn't well-known during his lifetime. Later, mathematicians dusted it off and realized how powerful it could be. Today it's celebrated as a key to understanding scientific confirmation and solving practical problems:

  • Scientists use Bayesian methods to weigh how strongly new evidence supports or undermines theories.
  • Email systems use it to filter spam by updating "beliefs" about whether a message looks spammy based on its contents.
  • Artificial intelligence uses Bayesian principles to "learn" from data and make decisions.
📚 PART 2 · THE THREE PIECES

① Priors

What you believe BEFORE (literally "prior to") any new evidence. Your starting credence. To find priors, you use what you already know — how common is one explanation vs. another in the world?

② Likelihood Ratio

How much more likely the evidence is under one explanation than another. This is the Question of Evidence (Lesson 7), written as a ratio.

③ Posteriors

What you believe AFTER updating your priors with the new evidence. Posteriors = Priors × Likelihood Ratio.

📚 PART 3 · TOM, THE MYSTERIOUS STUDENT

You want to know if Tom is a math PhD student or a business major (it's one of these two). The only thing you know is that Tom is shy. Let's work this through.

Priors (start with how common each group is)

Business majors massively outnumber math PhDs. Say 1,000 business students for every 1 math PhD.

Business : Math = 1,000 : 1

Without any evidence, it's 1,000× more likely Tom is a business major.

Likelihood Ratio for "Tom is shy"

Math PhDs are stereotypically shy — let's say 10% are noticeably shy. Business majors are usually more outgoing — say 1% noticeably shy.

Math : Business = 10 : 1    (so Business : Math = 1 : 10)

Shyness is 10× more likely under "math PhD" than under "business major."

Posteriors (multiply)

Business: 1,000 × 1 = 1,000
Math:       1 × 10 = 10
Posteriors = Business : Math = 1,000 : 10 = 100 : 1

After the shyness evidence, it's STILL 100× more likely Tom is a business major — just less overwhelmingly than before. The evidence pushed us toward "math PhD," but not enough to flip the conclusion.

📚 PART 4 · A SECOND PIECE OF EVIDENCE

Now suppose you see Tom carrying a book on Algebraic Topology — an advanced field of math far beyond what undergrads (let alone business majors) usually touch.

Key move: When you get NEW evidence, your old posteriors become your new priors. We don't start over from 1,000:1; we start from 100:1.

Step 1. New priors (= old posteriors)

Business : Math = 100 : 1

Step 2. Likelihood ratio for the Algebraic Topology book

~25% likely for a math PhD; ~5% likely for a business major. So 25:5 = 5:1 in favor of math.

Math : Business = 5 : 1    (so Business : Math = 1 : 5)

Step 3. New posteriors

Business: 100 × 1 = 100
Math:     1 × 5 = 5
Posteriors = Business : Math = 100 : 5 = 20 : 1

Even after TWO pieces of evidence pointing toward math PhD, business major is still 20× more likely. That's how powerful the original 1,000:1 prior was. To flip the conclusion you'd need a LOT more evidence.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · HOW AI ACTUALLY "LEARNS"
Many AI systems — including the spam filter on your email and large parts of how machine learning works — are doing some version of Bayesian updating under the hood. They have priors (what they "expected" before seeing your data) and they update with each new piece of evidence. When AI gets something wrong, often it's because its priors are stuck or its training data was lopsided. Humans have the same bug.
⚡ TRY ONE UPDATE
You think there's a 1:1 chance (50/50) that your friend is at the library vs. the cafeteria. Then you hear someone whisper "shh" — the library is 3× more likely to have shushing than the cafeteria. What are your posteriors (Library : Cafeteria)?
📚 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Bayesian Updating helps you adjust beliefs logically. Start with priors, weigh the evidence, update.
  2. The Question of Evidence is how you get a proper likelihood ratio.
  3. Even very strong evidence isn't enough on its own. If your priors are extreme, it takes a lot of evidence to flip the conclusion. Priors matter.
  4. When new evidence arrives, your old posteriors become your new priors. That's how you keep updating across many pieces of evidence.
