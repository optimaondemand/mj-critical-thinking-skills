📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Crack the code of how ideas are organized — and what non-paragraph elements can reveal.
Every time you read something, you're being handed a puzzle. The writer is trying to share ideas with you, but the real trick is in how those ideas are put together. The organization (structure) is the secret map to understanding what's most important. And sometimes, writers sneak in extra clues using diagrams, charts, images, and sidebars. Today you'll learn how to spot these hidden helpers and use them.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to identify the five common text structures.
2
I will be able to read visuals and non-paragraph elements as part of the author's message.
3
I will be able to map the structure of each curated source in my library.
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you read a textbook or article, do you usually…
📚 PART 1 · FIVE COMMON STRUCTURES

1. Description

"for example, such as, including, to illustrate"

2. Sequence / Order

"first, next, then, finally, before, after"

3. Cause and Effect

"because, since, as a result, due to, leads to"

4. Compare and Contrast

"similar, different, both, in contrast, unlike"

5. Problem and Solution

"problem, solution, solve, propose, suggest"

As you read, ask: What is the writer trying to do here? The answer usually reveals the structure.

📚 PART 2 · CRACKING THE STRUCTURE
  1. Hunt for clues. Signal words, headings, bullet points, numbered lists are neon signs telling you the structure.
  2. Map it out. Sketch how the ideas connect. Cause and effect → arrows. Compare/contrast → Venn diagram. Sequence → timeline.
  3. Ask: "What is the author's goal here?" Describing, explaining, comparing, or solving a problem? That zeroes you in.
⚡ TRY IT · IDENTIFY THE STRUCTURE
Match each opening to the structure it uses.
"Microplastics enter the ocean from many sources. As a result, marine animals consume them, which leads to…"
"While solar panels generate clean energy, they require rare-earth metals. In contrast, wind turbines…"
"Plastic waste is choking our oceans. One promising solution is…"
📚 PART 3 · THE POWER OF VISUALS

Not every clue is in the paragraphs. Authors use pictures, charts, tables, diagrams, sidebars, and pull quotes to help (or to persuade). These "non-paragraph elements" are often as important as the words.

Types you'll see: Charts and graphs · Diagrams · Tables · Photos/Illustrations · Sidebars/Boxes · Maps.

How to read visuals like a pro:

  1. Don't skip them. They're part of the message.
  2. Read captions and labels. Sometimes all the key info is there.
  3. Ask: "How does this connect to the main text? Is it support, an example, or a challenge?"
  4. Be skeptical. Does the visual really match what the author claims? Are any details exaggerated?
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI-GENERATED VISUALS
AI tools can produce charts and infographics that LOOK authoritative but contain made-up data, mislabeled axes, or invented sources. When you see a visual in an AI-generated piece — or any piece — check the underlying numbers and the source. A pretty chart is not the same as a true chart.
